# 3700 BCE: Megalithic Monuments

While Mesopotamia was developing toward urban civilization, the farming communities of Atlantic Europe were engaged in a remarkable parallel project: the construction of megalithic monuments. Dolmens, passage graves, stone circles, and long barrows were being built from Portugal to Scandinavia. These structures required the same ingredients as the later pyramids — surplus food to feed workers freed from farming, organized labor, skilled engineering, and a compelling ideological motivation — but they emerged in a context of dispersed village societies rather than cities and states.

The megalithic tradition represents one of the most striking cases of parallel cultural development in prehistory: unconnected societies from Ireland to Malta independently developed the practice of building enormous structures from stone, apparently for funerary and astronomical purposes.

## Key Developments

- **Megalithic tomb-building**: The Neolithic passage graves of Ireland (Newgrange area), the Orkney Islands, and Brittany are under construction around this period
- **Ötzi the Iceman's era**: The famous Alpine Iceman (found in 1991) lived around 3300 BCE, just after this century — he represents the Copper Age Alpine cultures of this period
- **Vinča culture**: The Vinča culture in the Balkans (c. 5700–4500 BCE) reaches its late phase; it had some of the earliest known proto-writing symbols in Europe
- **Copper Age Iberia**: The Los Millares culture in southeastern Spain develops copper smelting and fortified settlements
- **Eurasian steppe**: The Yamna culture begins forming on the Pontic-Caspian steppe — the likely origin of the Indo-European language family

## Newgrange and Astronomical Knowledge
The passage grave at Newgrange, Ireland (c. 3200 BCE, slightly after this century) was designed with extraordinary precision so that the winter solstice sunrise illuminates the inner burial chamber through a roofbox above the entrance. This required years of astronomical observation and precise architectural planning. It demonstrates that Neolithic European societies possessed sophisticated knowledge of celestial cycles long before they had writing.
