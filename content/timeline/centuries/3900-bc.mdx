# 3900 BCE: Proto-Urban Expansion

The century around 3900 BCE saw the expansion and intensification of the proto-urban cultures that would shortly produce the world's first true cities. The Ubaid cultural horizon — identifiable by its characteristic painted pottery — reached its maximum geographic extent, found from the Persian Gulf to the Anatolian plateau and along the Levantine coast. This wide distribution reflects not a single political entity but a network of interacting communities sharing material culture, religious forms, and trade connections.

The critical development of this century was demographic: population growth in the irrigated lowlands of Sumer was pushing settlements toward urban scale. When a community reaches several thousand inhabitants, governance can no longer rely on kinship and face-to-face relations alone. Specialized administrative institutions — the temple bureaucracy in Mesopotamia's case — begin to emerge as a solution to the coordination problem.

## Developments of the Era

- **Uruk expansion begins**: The city of Uruk, in southern Mesopotamia, starts its rapid growth that will make it the world's first metropolis
- **Egyptian Naqada I culture**: Along the Nile, increasingly elaborate burials signal growing social stratification; regional chiefs accumulate prestige goods
- **Caucasus metallurgy**: The Maykop culture in the northern Caucasus develops some of the most advanced metalworking of the period, including gold and silver objects
- **Wheeled vehicles**: The oldest direct evidence for wheeled vehicles (pictographs and wagon wheel impressions) dates to approximately 3900–3500 BCE in Mesopotamia and the Pontic steppe
- **Donkey domestication**: The domestic donkey appears in northeastern Africa around this time — a key pack animal for trade caravans

## The Significance of the Wheel
The invention of the wheel for transport (as distinct from the potter's wheel, which may be slightly older) was a landmark in human history. It dramatically reduced the energy required to move heavy loads, expanding the scale of possible construction projects and trade. Within a millennium, wheeled vehicles would spread from the Near East to India and Europe.
