# 3300 BCE: Late Uruk Period

The century around 3300 BCE represents the late phase of the Uruk period and the cusp of the transition from proto-writing to true writing. The pictographic signs on Uruk tablets were becoming more abstract and standardized — they were being pressed with a reed stylus rather than drawn, producing the wedge-shaped impressions that give cuneiform its name (Latin: *cuneus*, wedge). The sign repertoire was being extended from pure accounting notation toward a system capable of recording proper names and eventually any spoken word.

This was also the era of Ötzi the Iceman, a Copper Age Alpine man whose naturally mummified body was found in 1991 in a glacier on the Austro-Italian border. Dating to approximately 3300 BCE, Ötzi provides an extraordinary window into the material culture of this period: copper axe, bow and arrows, leather clothing, woven grass cloak, and evidence of a final meal of ibex, deer, einkorn wheat, and sloe berries before he was killed by an arrow from behind.

## Key Developments

- **Cuneiform development**: The Uruk script evolves from pictographic to increasingly abstract, standardized signs pressed with a reed stylus
- **Sign list expansion**: The number of distinct signs increases as scribes develop notation for names and more complex information
- **Ötzi the Iceman (c. 3300 BCE)**: The famous Alpine mummy represents the Copper Age cultures of central Europe
- **Proto-Elamite script**: A separate early writing system develops in Elam (southwestern Iran), possibly inspired by contact with Uruk
- **Naqada III in Egypt**: The final pre-dynastic culture, with clear signs of political centralization approaching state formation
- **Stonehenge Phase 1**: Construction begins at Stonehenge — a circular earthwork ditch and bank

## Ötzi and Copper Age Life
Ötzi's copper axe was of high-quality arsenical copper, freshly sharpened. His bow was of yew, his arrows of viburnum and dogwood. He had 64 tattoos — lines and crosses, mostly on joints — possibly therapeutic marks applied at acupuncture-like points. Genetic analysis shows he had brown eyes, dark skin, was lactose intolerant, and was predisposed to cardiovascular disease. He was approximately 45 years old when murdered. He represents a Copper Age Alpine farmer-herder, comfortable with technology but living in a world of considerable violence.
