# 500–1 BCE: Classical Greece, Alexander, and Rome

The five centuries from 500 to 1 BCE constitute the classical period of antiquity — the era that produced the cultural and intellectual foundations of Western civilization, the Hellenistic fusion of Greek and Eastern cultures, and the early Roman Republic that would become the greatest empire of the ancient world. These were also the centuries of the Buddha's disciples, Confucius's followers, and the crystallization of the great philosophical and religious systems.

The Persian Wars (490–479 BCE) began the period in dramatic fashion: a small collection of Greek city-states repelled the mighty Achaemenid Empire. Athens, elevated by this victory, entered its Golden Age under Pericles. The Parthenon was built. Herodotus and Thucydides invented history. Socrates, Plato, and Aristotle created philosophy. Then Alexander the Great destroyed the Persian Empire in a decade of brilliant campaigning, spreading Greek language and culture from Egypt to Central Asia.

## Key Developments

- **490 BCE**: Battle of Marathon — Athens defeats the Persian invasion force
- **480 BCE**: Battle of Thermopylae; 300 Spartans and allies delay Xerxes' army; Battle of Salamis; Greek fleet destroys the Persian fleet
- **461–429 BCE**: Age of Pericles — the Athenian Golden Age
- **447–432 BCE**: Construction of the Parthenon on the Athenian Acropolis
- **431–404 BCE**: Peloponnesian War — Athens vs Sparta; Athens eventually defeated
- **399 BCE**: Trial and execution of Socrates
- **c. 380 BCE**: Plato founds the Academy in Athens
- **356 BCE**: Birth of Alexander the Great
- **336–323 BCE**: Alexander's campaigns — he never loses a battle; conquers Persia, Egypt, Central Asia, and the Punjab
- **323 BCE**: Death of Alexander at Babylon, age 32
- **c. 300 BCE**: Euclid compiles *Elements* — the foundation of geometry
- **264–146 BCE**: The Punic Wars between Rome and Carthage; Rome emerges as master of the western Mediterranean
- **221 BCE**: Qin Shi Huang unifies China; builds the first Great Wall; standardizes script, weights, and measures
- **206 BCE**: Han Dynasty founded in China — lasting 400 years
- **c. 100–44 BCE**: Julius Caesar conquers Gaul; crosses the Rubicon; becomes dictator; is assassinated
- **27 BCE**: Augustus becomes first Roman Emperor; the Roman Republic ends

## The Athenian Experiment
Athenian democracy was radical and partial: all adult male citizens (excluding women, slaves, and foreigners) participated directly in governance. This was perhaps 30,000 men in a city of 250,000. Yet the democratic culture it produced — the value of debate, the accountability of leaders, the prestige of persuasion — generated intellectual achievements out of all proportion to Athens' size. The Athenians invented not just philosophy but also formal history, systematic biology, theoretical mathematics, and tragedy.

## Alexander and Hellenism
Alexander's conquests had cultural consequences that outlasted his empire. The Koine Greek language became the common tongue of the eastern Mediterranean for centuries, and it was in this language that the New Testament would be written 300 years later. Alexandria, the city he founded in Egypt, became the intellectual capital of the ancient world with its famous Library and Museum.
