# 1000–500 BCE: Iron Age and the Axial Age

The five centuries from 1000 to 500 BCE were a period of recovery, reorganization, and an extraordinary flowering of new ideas. Iron metallurgy — cheaper and more widely distributed than bronze — democratized weapons and tools. The collapse of the Bronze Age palace economies had destroyed old hierarchies and created space for new political forms: the Greek polis, the Hebrew kingdom, the Persian tribal confederacy.

This was also the beginning of the "Axial Age," a concept coined by the philosopher Karl Jaspers to describe the near-simultaneous emergence, in China, India, Persia, and the Mediterranean, of a new kind of reflective, ethical, universalizing thought that questioned traditional mythological frameworks. The prophets of Israel, Zoroaster, and the earliest Greek philosophers all flourished in this half-millennium.

## Key Developments

- **c. 1000 BCE**: David establishes Jerusalem as capital of the United Kingdom of Israel
- **c. 900–700 BCE**: The Neo-Assyrian Empire becomes the most powerful state in the ancient Near East; pioneers systematic deportation of conquered peoples
- **c. 850 BCE**: The oldest surviving alphabetic inscription (Mesha Stele) in a Semitic language
- **c. 800 BCE**: The Greek alphabet is adapted from Phoenician — the ancestor of all Western alphabets
- **776 BCE**: First recorded Olympic Games at Olympia
- **c. 750 BCE**: Homer's *Iliad* and *Odyssey* composed or written down
- **c. 700 BCE**: Hesiod composes the *Theogony* and *Works and Days* — foundational Greek texts
- **722 BCE**: Assyria destroys the Northern Kingdom of Israel; the Ten Tribes are scattered
- **c. 628 BCE**: Birth of Zoroaster (traditional date); the Persian prophet founds a dualistic monotheism
- **c. 620 BCE**: Draco codifies Athenian law — so harsh it gives us the word "Draconian"
- **612 BCE**: Babylon and Media destroy Nineveh; the Assyrian Empire ends
- **594 BCE**: Solon's reforms in Athens — the beginning of Athenian democracy
- **586 BCE**: Nebuchadnezzar II destroys Jerusalem and deports the Jews to Babylon — the Babylonian Exile
- **c. 560 BCE**: Cyrus the Great begins his rise to power in Persia
- **c. 550 BCE**: Cyrus founds the Achaemenid Persian Empire

## The Hebrew Bible in Formation
The Babylonian Exile (586–538 BCE) was the crucible of the Hebrew Bible as we know it. Cut off from Temple worship and facing the question of why God had allowed this catastrophe, Judean scribes and priests compiled, edited, and composed the texts that became the Torah and the historical books. The Exile produced both the sharpest crisis and the most creative period in Israelite religious history, transforming a tribal national religion into a portable, text-centered monotheism capable of surviving without land or temple.

## The Greek Alphabet Revolution
The adaptation of the Phoenician alphabet by the Greeks around 800 BCE had consequences far beyond Greece. Unlike cuneiform and hieroglyphics, alphabetic writing required no years of scribal training — anyone could learn it. This democratized literacy, made poetry and philosophy accessible without a professional scribal class, and ultimately enabled the printing press revolution three thousand years later.
