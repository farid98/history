# 500–1000 CE: Islam, the Franks, and Tang China

The five centuries from 500 to 1000 CE were among the most consequential in world history. The birth and expansion of Islam reshaped the political and religious map of the Old World, creating a unified cultural zone from Spain to Central Asia within a century. In China, the Tang Dynasty presided over what many historians consider the most culturally sophisticated and materially wealthy civilization on earth. In the remnant of the Western Roman world, Germanic kings and the Catholic Church forged a new Christian civilization, culminating in Charlemagne's Carolingian Empire. And from Scandinavia, the Vikings exploded outward to raid and settle from Newfoundland to Constantinople.

The Justinianic Plague (541–549 CE), the first documented pandemic of bubonic plague, killed perhaps 25–50 million people across the Byzantine Empire and Sassanid Persia, contributing to both empires' weakening and creating the conditions for the Arab conquests that followed.

## Key Developments

- **570 CE**: Birth of Muhammad in Mecca
- **610 CE**: Muhammad begins receiving revelations; the Quran is recited
- **622 CE**: The Hijra — Muhammad and followers flee to Medina; Year 1 of the Islamic calendar
- **632 CE**: Death of Muhammad; Abu Bakr becomes the first Caliph
- **636 CE**: Battle of al-Qadisiyyah; Arab armies defeat the Sassanid Persian Empire
- **637 CE**: Arab conquest of Jerusalem
- **642 CE**: Arab conquest of Egypt; the Library of Alexandria's final remnants are dispersed
- **711 CE**: Arab forces cross into Spain (Hispania); within a decade they control most of the Iberian Peninsula
- **618 CE**: Tang Dynasty founded; Chang'an becomes the world's largest city
- **732 CE**: Battle of Tours/Poitiers — Charles Martel halts the Arab advance into France
- **750 CE**: Abbasid Caliphate replaces the Umayyads; capital moves to Baghdad
- **762 CE**: Baghdad founded; the House of Wisdom established
- **793 CE**: Vikings raid Lindisfarne — the beginning of the Viking Age in earnest
- **800 CE**: Pope Leo III crowns Charlemagne Holy Roman Emperor
- **c. 868 CE**: The Diamond Sutra is printed in China — the world's oldest dated printed book
- **907 CE**: Tang Dynasty falls; Five Dynasties period in China

## The Arab Conquests
The speed of the early Islamic conquests remains astonishing to historians. Within a decade of Muhammad's death, Arab armies had overthrown the Persian Empire (637 CE) and conquered Syria, Palestine, and Egypt from Byzantium. Within a century they had reached the Pyrenees in the west and the Indus in the east. Military prowess accounts for some of this success, but so does the exhaustion of the Byzantine and Sassanid empires by their decades of mutually destructive warfare, and the genuine appeal of Islam's monotheist message and its relative tolerance of "People of the Book."

## Tang Dynasty China
The Tang Dynasty (618–907 CE) created a cosmopolitan civilization of extraordinary sophistication. Chang'an, the capital, had a population of perhaps one million — the world's largest city — with streets laid out on a grid, foreign merchant quarters, Buddhist and Taoist temples, and a culture open to influences from Central Asia, India, and Persia. Tang poetry, considered the zenith of Chinese literary achievement, produced Li Bai and Du Fu. Block printing emerged, enabling the mass reproduction of texts.
