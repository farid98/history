# 1000–1 BCE: Classical Antiquity

The first millennium BCE produced an astonishing concentration of intellectual, spiritual, and political innovation that the philosopher Karl Jaspers termed the "Axial Age." Within a few centuries — roughly 800 to 200 BCE — independent thinkers in China, India, Persia, and the Mediterranean world simultaneously began to question traditional religious assumptions and develop systematic philosophy, ethics, and metaphysics. Confucius, the Buddha, Zoroaster, Socrates, and the Hebrew prophets all lived within a few centuries of each other without knowledge of one another. Their ideas would shape the majority of humanity ever after.

This was also the age of iron. The collapse of Bronze Age trade networks had forced communities to develop local iron-smelting; iron's superiority for tools and weapons transformed both agriculture and warfare. The first great empires of the Iron Age — Assyria, Persia, Mauryan India, Qin China — deployed unprecedented military and administrative power.

## Key Events

- **c. 1000 BCE**: Iron Age spreads across Europe, Near East, and Sub-Saharan Africa
- **c. 900 BCE**: Homer's *Iliad* and *Odyssey* are composed (or written down), founding the Western literary tradition
- **c. 800 BCE**: Greek city-states (poleis) begin to develop; the first Olympic Games are held in 776 BCE
- **c. 750 BCE**: Rise of the Assyrian Empire under Tiglath-Pileser III, the first state with a professional standing army
- **c. 604 BCE**: Nebuchadnezzar II of Babylon destroys Jerusalem; the Jews are exiled to Babylon — a pivotal moment for monotheism
- **c. 563 BCE**: Birth of Siddhartha Gautama (the Buddha) in Nepal
- **c. 551 BCE**: Birth of Confucius in Lu (China)
- **c. 509 BCE**: Rome expels its kings and establishes the Republic
- **c. 490–479 BCE**: Persian Wars — Greece repels the Achaemenid Persian Empire at Marathon, Thermopylae, and Salamis
- **c. 447 BCE**: Construction of the Parthenon begins in Athens
- **c. 336–323 BCE**: Alexander the Great conquers the Persian Empire and extends Hellenic culture to Central Asia and Egypt
- **c. 221 BCE**: Qin Shi Huang unifies China for the first time, standardizes weights, measures, and script
- **c. 146 BCE**: Rome destroys Carthage and Corinth; the Mediterranean becomes a Roman lake
- **c. 44 BCE**: Julius Caesar is assassinated; the Roman Republic begins its transformation into the Empire

## Major Civilizations

### The Greek World
The Greek polis was a unique political invention: a self-governing community of free male citizens who deliberated on public matters in person. Athens carried this furthest with democracy. Sparta took it in an authoritarian direction. Between them they generated the philosophic, scientific, and artistic traditions that became the foundation of Western civilization. Plato, Aristotle, Thucydides, Hippocrates, Euclid, and Archimedes all worked in this period.

### The Persian Empire
Founded by Cyrus the Great (r. 559–530 BCE), the Achaemenid Persian Empire became the largest the world had yet seen, extending from Greece to the Indus. Its genius lay in administrative tolerance: subject peoples were permitted their own laws, religions, and languages. Cyrus famously freed the Jews from Babylonian captivity. The Royal Road from Sardis to Susa enabled rapid communication across 2,700 kilometers.

### Rome
From a small city on the Tiber, Rome developed the concept of a citizen-soldier republic governed by law. By 100 BCE it controlled the entire Mediterranean basin, absorbing Greek culture while developing its own genius for law, engineering, and administration. Roman roads, aqueducts, and legal codes would structure European civilization for two thousand years.

## Legacy

The first millennium BCE established the intellectual and institutional frameworks — philosophy, monotheism, democracy, codified law, imperial administration — that would define human civilization in both East and West for the next two thousand years.
