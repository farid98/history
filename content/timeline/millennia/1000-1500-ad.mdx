# 1000–1500 CE: The Medieval World

The five centuries from 1000 to 1500 CE encompass what many historians consider the "High" and "Late" Middle Ages in Europe, but also the zenith of Islamic civilization, the golden age of Song China, the most dramatic demographic catastrophe in recorded history (the Black Death), and the emergence of the great Mesoamerican and Andean empires. The period ends precisely at the hinge of European history: on the verge of transoceanic expansion that would connect, for the first time, all inhabited continents into a single global system.

This era was one of paradoxes. Gothic cathedrals soared to unprecedented heights while the Black Death killed one-third of Europe's population. Universities were founded and Aristotle was rediscovered while the Crusades inflicted immense violence. The Mongols destroyed the Abbasid Caliphate and the Song Dynasty but their Pax Mongolica also opened the Silk Road to unprecedented commerce.

## Key Events

- **1000 CE**: Leif Erikson reaches North America (Vinland) — five centuries before Columbus
- **1054 CE**: The Great Schism divides Christianity into Roman Catholic and Eastern Orthodox
- **1066 CE**: Norman Conquest of England; William the Conqueror transforms English language and governance
- **1095 CE**: Pope Urban II calls the First Crusade
- **1099 CE**: Crusaders capture Jerusalem in a bloodbath
- **1206 CE**: Genghis Khan unifies the Mongol tribes and begins the world's largest land conquest
- **1215 CE**: Magna Carta signed in England, laying the groundwork for constitutional governance
- **1258 CE**: Mongols sack Baghdad, destroying the Abbasid Caliphate and the House of Wisdom
- **1271–1295 CE**: Marco Polo travels to the court of Kublai Khan
- **1325 CE**: Founding of Tenochtitlan — capital of the Aztec Empire
- **1347–1351 CE**: The Black Death kills 30–60% of Europe's population, 75–200 million worldwide
- **1368 CE**: Ming Dynasty overthrows Mongol Yuan Dynasty in China
- **1405–1433 CE**: Zheng He's voyages — China sends massive fleets as far as East Africa
- **1438 CE**: The Inca Empire (Tawantinsuyu) begins rapid expansion in South America
- **1453 CE**: Ottomans take Constantinople, ending the Byzantine Empire
- **1492 CE**: Columbus reaches the Americas; Reconquista completes the Christian reconquest of Spain

## Major Civilizations

### The Mongol World-Empire
Genghis Khan (r. 1206–1227 CE) and his successors created the largest contiguous land empire in history, stretching from Korea to Hungary. The Mongol conquests were catastrophic — the destruction of Central Asian cities like Samarkand and Baghdad ended centuries of urban civilization. Yet the subsequent Pax Mongolica (Mongol Peace) enabled the safe passage of merchants, diplomats, and diseases across Eurasia. The Black Death traveled west along these routes.

### The Islamic Golden Age
From approximately 800 to 1300 CE, Islamic civilization led the world in mathematics, astronomy, medicine, and philosophy. Scholars in Baghdad, Cairo, Cordoba, and Samarkand preserved and extended Greek learning. Algebra, algorithms, optics, and the decimal system were either invented or significantly developed by Islamic scholars. The Crusades and Mongol invasions fractured this intellectual world.

### Medieval Europe's New Institutions
Despite — or because of — the political fragmentation of the post-Roman West, Europe developed institutions of remarkable durability: universities (Bologna, 1088; Oxford, c. 1096; Paris, c. 1150), chartered towns with legal rights, the common law tradition in England, and parliamentary assemblies. The Black Death, paradoxically, accelerated economic change by dramatically raising the bargaining power of surviving laborers.

## Legacy

The period ended with Europe on the verge of oceanic expansion, equipped with Arabic numerals, the printing press (Gutenberg, c. 1440), gunpowder weapons, and navigational knowledge borrowed from Islamic and Chinese sources. The unification of the globe that followed would be shaped by the power dynamics and diseases that medieval Eurasia had developed.
