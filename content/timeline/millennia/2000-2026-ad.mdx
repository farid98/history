# 2000–2026 CE: The Digital Age

The first quarter of the 21st century has been defined by the explosive maturation of digital technology and the simultaneous fracturing of the post-Cold War international order. The internet, mobile computing, and social media transformed how humans communicate, consume, and organize, while also amplifying polarization, disinformation, and surveillance. The world grew richer on average, with hundreds of millions escaping extreme poverty in Asia, while inequality within wealthy nations reached levels not seen since the Gilded Age.

The period was also scarred by catastrophes: the September 11 attacks and the resulting "War on Terror," the 2008 global financial crisis, the COVID-19 pandemic that killed over 7 million people and disrupted global society for three years, and the accelerating climate crisis. By 2026, the rise of large language models and generative artificial intelligence had introduced a technology with the potential to rival or surpass human cognitive labor across nearly every domain.

## Key Events

- **September 11, 2001**: Al-Qaeda attacks on the United States kill 2,977 people; the subsequent "War on Terror" reshapes global geopolitics
- **2001 CE**: Wikipedia launched; the open-knowledge revolution begins
- **2003 CE**: Human Genome Project completed — the full sequence of human DNA
- **2004 CE**: Mark Zuckerberg launches Facebook; the social media era begins
- **2007 CE**: iPhone launched; the smartphone era begins
- **2008 CE**: Global financial crisis — the worst since the Great Depression
- **2008 CE**: Barack Obama elected as first Black President of the United States
- **2010 CE**: Arab Spring — popular uprisings across the Middle East and North Africa
- **2012 CE**: Higgs boson discovered at CERN, completing the Standard Model of particle physics
- **2015 CE**: Paris Climate Agreement — 196 nations commit to limiting global warming
- **2016 CE**: Brexit referendum; Donald Trump elected US president — populist nationalism rises globally
- **2019 CE**: COVID-19 emerges in Wuhan, China
- **2020 CE**: COVID-19 declared a pandemic; global lockdowns; over 7 million deaths
- **2022 CE**: Russia invades Ukraine — the largest European land war since 1945
- **2022 CE**: ChatGPT launched; the AI revolution becomes publicly visible
- **2023 CE**: GPT-4, Gemini, Claude — large language models demonstrate near-human performance across knowledge domains
- **2024 CE**: AlphaFold solves the protein folding problem; AI begins transforming drug discovery
- **2025 CE**: AI agents begin to automate knowledge work at scale

## Major Developments

### The Digital Revolution
The internet has penetrated to over 5 billion people, fundamentally transforming commerce, communication, entertainment, and politics. Google, Amazon, Apple, Microsoft, and Meta became among the most valuable companies in history, concentrating data and economic power on an unprecedented scale. Social media platforms with billions of users created new forms of community and new vectors for polarization and manipulation.

### Geopolitical Transformation
The post-Cold War "unipolar moment" of American hegemony gave way to an increasingly multipolar world. China's GDP, effectively zero in 1990 in per-capita terms, grew to challenge the United States as the world's largest economy by purchasing power parity. Russia under Putin pursued a revisionist foreign policy that culminated in the full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022. The rise of nationalist and populist movements in Europe and the Americas challenged the liberal international order that had prevailed since 1945.

### Climate Change
The atmospheric concentration of CO₂ crossed 400 parts per million in 2013 — the highest level in at least 3 million years. Global average temperatures in 2023–2025 exceeded pre-industrial levels by more than 1.5°C for the first time, crossing the threshold identified by the Paris Agreement as dangerous. Extreme weather events — floods, droughts, wildfires, and hurricanes — intensified and multiplied, displacing hundreds of millions of people.

### The AI Revolution
The development of transformer-based large language models, beginning with GPT-3 (2020) and accelerating through GPT-4, Claude, and Gemini, represented a step change in artificial intelligence. For the first time, software systems demonstrated fluent natural language ability, coding competence, and broad reasoning that had previously required human intelligence. By 2025, AI was beginning to automate significant portions of knowledge work. The long-term consequences for labor, education, creativity, and governance remained deeply uncertain.

## Legacy in Progress

The first quarter of the 21st century ended with humanity facing simultaneous transformational challenges: climate change, the concentration of AI capability, great-power competition, pandemic risk, and deepening inequality. Whether these challenges will produce catastrophe, adaptation, or transformative human flourishing remains the open question that future historians will answer.
