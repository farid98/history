# 1–1000 CE: Late Antiquity and the Early Middle Ages

The first millennium of the Common Era opened with the Roman Empire at its height — a single political entity encircling the Mediterranean — and closed with Europe fragmented among feudal kingdoms, Islam spanning from Spain to Central Asia, and the Tang Dynasty presiding over the world's wealthiest and most sophisticated civilization. In between lay transformations of enormous consequence: the birth and spread of Christianity; the fall of Rome in the West; the emergence of Islam; the rise of the Byzantine and Sassanid Persian empires; and the first great expansion of the Turkic, Mongol, and Norse peoples.

The millennium was also one of plague and demographic crisis. The Antonine Plague (165–180 CE), the Plague of Cyprian (249–262 CE), and the Justinianic Plague (541–549 CE) — the first documented pandemic of bubonic plague — together killed tens of millions and contributed to the collapse of late antique urban society.

## Key Events

- **c. 4 BCE – 30 CE**: Life of Jesus of Nazareth; crucifixion by order of Pontius Pilate
- **70 CE**: Roman general Titus destroys the Jerusalem Temple; the Jewish Diaspora accelerates
- **79 CE**: Mount Vesuvius erupts, destroying Pompeii and Herculaneum
- **c. 105 CE**: Cai Lun refines papermaking in China — one of history's most consequential inventions
- **313 CE**: Constantine issues the Edict of Milan, legalizing Christianity in the Roman Empire
- **410 CE**: Visigoths sack Rome — the first time in 800 years the city had fallen to an enemy
- **476 CE**: Romulus Augustulus, last Western Roman Emperor, deposed by Odoacer
- **570 CE**: Birth of Muhammad in Mecca
- **622 CE**: Muhammad's Hijra to Medina — the founding moment of the Islamic community (umma)
- **632–750 CE**: The Arab conquests carry Islam from Spain to the borders of India and China in a century
- **618 CE**: Tang Dynasty founded in China — a cosmopolitan golden age of poetry, trade, and technology
- **793 CE**: Vikings raid the monastery of Lindisfarne — beginning of the Viking Age
- **800 CE**: Charlemagne crowned Holy Roman Emperor by Pope Leo III
- **960 CE**: Song Dynasty founded in China, inaugurating a period of commercial revolution

## Major Civilizations

### Christianity and the Roman Transformation
Jesus was executed as a minor Jewish dissident under Tiberius. Within three centuries his followers controlled the Roman state. The conversion of Constantine (c. 312 CE) and Theodosius's declaration of Christianity as the state religion (380 CE) transformed both Christianity and Rome. The church became a vehicle for Roman administrative continuity in the West after the political collapse of 476 CE.

### The Islamic World
The speed of the early Islamic conquests is almost without parallel. Within a century of Muhammad's death, Arabic-speaking Muslim warriors had conquered the Sassanid Persian Empire, most of the Byzantine territories in the Levant and North Africa, the Iberian Peninsula, and had reached the borders of India and Tang China. The Abbasid Caliphate (750–1258) created a zone of intellectual vitality — the House of Wisdom in Baghdad translated Greek, Persian, and Indian scientific texts — that preserved and extended classical knowledge through Europe's Dark Ages.

### Byzantium
The Eastern Roman Empire, with its capital at Constantinople, survived for another thousand years after Rome fell. Greek-speaking, Christian, and ferociously administered, Byzantium preserved Roman law, Greek learning, and Christian theology. Under Justinian (r. 527–565 CE), it briefly reconquered North Africa and Italy, and codified Roman law in the *Corpus Juris Civilis*, which became the basis of most European legal systems.

## Legacy

This millennium established the three great Abrahamic religions in their definitive forms and distributions, created the political patchwork of medieval Europe, and saw the center of global civilization shift from the Mediterranean to the Islamic world. The technologies developed in China — paper, printing, gunpowder, the compass — would eventually transform the West.
